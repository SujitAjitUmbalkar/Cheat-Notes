
# Concept – Kafka Log Retention

---

## What is Log Retention?

Kafka **does not delete a message immediately after it is consumed**.

Instead, Kafka keeps messages for a configured period of time (or until a size limit is reached). 

---

## Why?

Suppose:

```text
Offset 0
Offset 1
Offset 2
Offset 3
```

Consumer reads all events.

Normally, you might think Kafka deletes them.

❌ Wrong.

Kafka keeps them.

This allows:

* Another consumer to read them later.
* A consumer to reprocess old events.
* Recovery after failures.

---

## How long are messages kept?

Kafka deletes messages based on **retention policies**, such as:

* Time-based retention.
* Size-based retention. 

---

## Example

Suppose retention is **7 days**.

```text
Day 1  → Event Stored
Day 2  → Consumer Reads
Day 5  → Event Still Exists
Day 7  → Event Expires
Day 8  → Kafka Deletes It
```

Consumption **does not** trigger deletion.

---

# Notes

* Kafka retains records even after they are consumed.
* Messages are deleted according to the configured **retention policy**, not after consumption.
* Retention can be based on **time** or **log size**.
* Log retention allows replaying or reprocessing old events. 

---

# Remember ⭐

* **Consumed ≠ Deleted**
* **Offsets track consumer progress; retention controls when Kafka removes data.**
* Kafka is an **event log**, not a queue that removes messages immediately after reading.
